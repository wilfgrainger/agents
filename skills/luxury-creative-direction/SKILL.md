# Luxury Creative Direction

## Overview

Create a decisive website blueprint before pixels or components multiply. The goal is not merely to make a site look expensive; it is to align brand meaning, audience psychology, competitive differentiation, visual language, narrative flow, and technical feasibility.

## Required Inputs

Use available evidence first. Identify:
- brand, offer, category, and commercial goal
- primary audience and buying context
- competitors or reference sites to outperform
- desired first-three-second emotional response
- existing brand assets and constraints
- known platform, CMS, performance, accessibility, or delivery constraints

If information is missing, make explicit assumptions rather than inventing facts.

## Workflow

1. **Brand diagnosis**
   - Distill the brand into 3–5 traits.
   - Separate genuine brand signals from generic “luxury” decoration.
   - Define what the site must never feel like.

2. **Audience and competitive analysis**
   - Describe visitor intent, sophistication, anxieties, and expectations.
   - Identify competitor conventions worth preserving and clichés worth rejecting.
   - State the specific experience advantage this site should own.

3. **Three-second experience**
   - Define the immediate visual hierarchy, emotional impression, primary promise, and first action.
   - Ensure the visitor can identify who the site is for and why it matters without scrolling.

4. **Visual identity**
   Define:
   - typography roles
   - colour logic
   - spacing and density
   - imagery/art direction
   - grid and composition
   - surface, border, icon, and control treatment
   - rules for restraint and contrast

5. **Page and scroll story**
   - Map the page section-by-section.
   - Give every section one job.
   - State the visitor question answered at each stage.
   - Remove sections that exist only to fill space.

6. **Motion and technology direction**
   - Describe only motion that supports hierarchy, causality, feedback, or narrative.
   - Recommend technologies based on product needs, not fashion.

7. **Build order**
   Sequence work by dependency and risk:
   - content/IA
   - design system
   - critical above-fold experience
   - reusable sections
   - responsive states
   - motion
   - performance/accessibility
   - polish

## Output Contract

Return:
1. Brand and audience diagnosis
2. Competitive positioning
3. Three-second experience statement
4. Visual identity system
5. Page structure
6. Scroll narrative
7. Motion principles
8. Technology recommendation
9. Ordered implementation plan
10. Five non-negotiable design rules

## Quality Bar

Reject:
- interchangeable “premium” templates
- excessive gradients, glass effects, gold, or animation used as luxury shorthand
- unexplained technology choices
- desktop-only compositions
- visual flourish that competes with comprehension

Luxury should come from confidence, editing, rhythm, material quality, and deliberate hierarchy.
